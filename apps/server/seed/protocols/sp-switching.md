## When this applies

- Opening or closing breakers, disconnect switches, fused switches and switchgear; racking draw-out breakers; resetting a tripped breaker or replacing a blown fuse; re-energizing after work.
- Transfer switches also follow the site's generator procedure and R-EMERG-01.
- Switching inside the arc-flash boundary is a task under R-PPE-01: wear the PPE the label requires.

## Hazards

- Arc flash during operation, especially when closing onto a fault, racking, or operating gear that is damaged, poorly maintained or wet.
- Re-energizing a fault by resetting a device that tripped on a short circuit or ground fault.
- Opening a device not rated to interrupt load current.
- Energizing equipment that someone is still working on.
- Unplanned loss of life-safety, standby or healthcare loads.

## PPE & tools

- Kestrel minimum plus label PPE for any switching inside the arc-flash boundary (R-PPE-01).
- The manufacturer's racking tool or a remote racking device; a remote operator where the site provides one.
- Insulated fuse pullers rated for the voltage; replacement fuses of identical type, class and rating.
- Flashlight, CAT III/IV meter, and the site file's one-line diagram.

## Procedure

1. Know why you are switching, what the device feeds and who is affected. Give notice per R-NOTIFY-01. For life-safety, standby and healthcare loads follow R-EMERG-01 and R-HEALTH-01. Never manually bypass a transfer switch without supervisor approval (R-EMERG-01).
2. Check the label is valid and wear its PPE. If it is missing, illegible or expired, escalate (R-ESC-01) and operate an upstream device with a valid label instead where possible.
3. Inspect before you touch: doors closed and latched, covers and fasteners in place, and no overheating, burning smell, buzzing or arcing sounds, water or physical damage. If any are present, do not operate the device; escalate (R-ESC-01).
4. Stand beside the enclosure on the handle side, not in front of the door. Use the hand nearest the handle, turn your face away, and keep others outside the arc-flash boundary.
5. Reduce load first: switch off downstream loads or open feeder breakers before opening a main or disconnect. Interrupt load only with load-rated switches or breakers, never with non-load-break switches, fuses or connectors except in an emergency (1910.334).
6. Operate with one firm, complete motion.
7. Racking: confirm the breaker is open, keep doors closed where the design allows, use the remote racking device or the manufacturer's tool at full length, follow the manufacturer's instructions, and never defeat an interlock.
8. Tripped device: a trip means something happened. Before any reset:
   - Find out what was running and when it tripped; read the trip-unit indicators.
   - Inspect the device, panel and load for damage, heat, smell or water.
   - If there are signs of a short circuit or ground fault, or you cannot tell an overload from a fault, isolate under sp-loto and test the circuit and load first.
   - Reset only once you have determined the circuit and equipment can be safely energized (1910.334). Move the handle fully to off or reset, then on.
9. One reset only. If it trips again, do not reset again: lock it out, find the cause, and tell the customer what you have verified (R-CUST-01). Repeated resets and repeated fuse replacement are prohibited.
10. Never install a larger or different type of fuse or breaker to stop trips.
11. Re-energizing after work: all tools, jumpers and grounds removed, covers on, everyone warned and clear, every lock removed by its owner (R-LOTO-01). Close upstream devices first, then feeders, then loads.
12. When a main or feeder device clears a fault, report it to your supervisor: the device may need inspection or testing before it returns to service (R-ESC-01).

## Verification

- Device position confirmed by its indicator and, where it matters, by meter.
- Expected loads restored and the customer informed.
- Trip cause identified before reset, or the circuit left locked out.

## Stop-work triggers

- Missing, illegible or expired label (R-PPE-01).
- Signs of overheating, damage or water (R-ESC-01).
- A device that trips again after one reset.
- Standing in water or on a wet floor (sp-wet-locations).
- A switching order you do not understand, or one affecting life-safety loads without the approvals above.
- Any request to bypass a transfer switch or defeat an interlock.

## Records

- Devices operated, times, trip-unit indications, readings and trip cause in the work order (R-DOC-01).
- Photos of labels and of any gear opened (R-DOC-01).
- Notifications and acknowledgements (R-NOTIFY-01).
