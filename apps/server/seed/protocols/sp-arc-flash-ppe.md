## When this applies

- Before any task inside the arc-flash boundary or the limited approach boundary: opening covers or doors on energized gear, voltage testing (including the absence-of-voltage test), thermography with covers off, switching and racking.
- Every time you arrive at a piece of equipment: read its label first.

## Hazards

- Arc flash: burns, blast pressure, molten metal, shrapnel, hearing damage and intense light. Incident energy rises with available fault current and clearing time and falls with distance.
- Shock: contact with exposed live parts directly or through a conductive tool.
- Stale data: a label that no longer matches the system after changes to the utility supply, transformer, protective devices or settings.

## PPE & tools

- Kestrel minimum on every site (R-PPE-01): safety glasses, hard hat, arc-rated long-sleeve shirt of at least 8 cal/cm², and voltage-rated gloves with leather protectors for any testing.
- Arc-rated clothing is the outer layer; underlayers are natural fiber or arc-rated, never meltable synthetics. Sleeves down, fronts fastened.
- PPE category minimum arc ratings: category 1 is 4 cal/cm², category 2 is 8, category 3 is 25, category 4 is 40 (NFPA 70E Art. 130).
- Every category: arc-rated shirt and pants or coverall at the category rating, hard hat, safety glasses and hearing protection (ear canal inserts). Category 1 adds an arc-rated face shield; category 2 an arc-rated face shield with balaclava, or a flash suit hood, plus leather footwear; categories 3 and 4 a flash suit hood and jacket and pants to the rating, with arc-rated gloves or rubber gloves with leather protectors.
- Kestrel's 8 cal/cm² shirt alone does not meet category 2.
- Rubber gloves: choose a class whose maximum use voltage covers the system (class 00 up to 500 V AC, class 0 up to 1,000 V AC). Air test before each day's use; electrical test every six months (1910.137).
- Class E hard hat. Inspect arc-rated clothing for contamination, tears and non-arc-rated repairs.

## Procedure

1. Find the label and confirm it belongs to this equipment (the ID matches the site file).
2. Check the date. If the label is missing, illegible, undated or dated more than 5 years ago, go to step 8.
3. Read the nominal voltage, arc-flash boundary, working distance, and either the incident energy in cal/cm² or the PPE category, plus the shock boundaries and glove class if shown.
4. Select PPE:
   - Incident energy given: every arc-rated layer or system must be rated at or above that value.
   - PPE category given: wear the full PPE for that category.
   - Never choose PPE from memory, from neighbouring gear, or from what the last crew wore.
5. Apply the shock boundaries from the label. For common AC systems, NFPA 70E Art. 130 sets, for 151 to 750 V, a limited approach boundary of 3 ft 6 in to fixed exposed parts and a restricted approach boundary of 1 ft; for 50 to 150 V the restricted boundary is avoid contact.
   - Unqualified people stay outside the limited approach boundary unless a qualified person escorts and briefs them.
   - Only qualified persons in shock PPE cross the restricted approach boundary, and without an EEWP only for diagnostics (R-EWP-01).
6. Barricade at the larger of the arc-flash and limited approach boundaries; post an attendant if barricades are not enough (1910.335).
7. If your face or body will be closer than the label's working distance, or the incident energy exceeds 40 cal/cm², stop: no PPE category covers it, and the task is planned de-energized with the supervisor (R-ESC-01).
8. Missing, illegible, undated or expired label:
   - Do not open, test or work on the equipment energized (R-PPE-01).
   - Photograph the label area and record the equipment ID (R-DOC-01).
   - Escalate to your supervisor (R-ESC-01) and tell the customer factually that the label must be updated before work (R-CUST-01).
   - Only the supervisor, using an updated study or an engineering determination, sets interim PPE in writing, and the equipment is relabeled.
9. Treat a label as suspect and escalate if the equipment, its upstream protective device or settings, or the utility service have changed since the label date.

## Verification

- Label read and valid; PPE rated at or above the label value.
- Glove class matches the voltage and the test date is current.
- Boundaries barricaded before any cover comes off.

## Stop-work triggers

- Missing, illegible, undated or expired label (R-PPE-01, R-ESC-01).
- Incident energy above 40 cal/cm², or a working distance you cannot keep.
- Damaged, contaminated or out-of-date PPE or gloves.
- Customer or schedule pressure to skip PPE or "just take a quick reading".
- Any sign of impending failure: heat, smell, noise, water or damage (R-ESC-01).

## Records

- Equipment ID, label date, incident energy or category, and PPE worn, in the work order (R-DOC-01).
- Photo of the label with every opened panel (R-DOC-01).
- Label problems reported to the supervisor, with the time of the call.
