## When this applies

- Any work on, or that could affect, the essential electrical system (EES) of a healthcare facility: life safety, critical and equipment branches, their transfer switches, panels, feeders and receptacles.
- Isolated power panels and line isolation monitors (LIMs) in operating rooms and wet procedure locations.
- Normal-power work in patient-care spaces, or upstream work that could trip an EES feeder.
- Background: NFPA 99, NEC Art. 517 and Art. 700. Generator and ATS work also follows sp-generator-ats.

## Hazards

- Patient harm: a lost circuit can stop ventilators, anesthesia machines, infusion pumps, monitors or surgical lights. Not every device has a battery.
- Life safety branch loss: egress lighting, exit signs, fire alarm, medical gas alarms. Equipment branch loss: procedure-room ventilation, medical vacuum and air.
- Mixed feeds: patient-care rooms usually have both normal and critical-branch circuits. EES receptacles are distinctively marked (often red); confirm the branch from panel labels and the site file, never from color alone.
- Isolated power is ungrounded. A LIM alarm means one fault has already removed isolation; a second fault can trip the circuit or shock someone.
- Infection control: ceiling and wall work releases dust into clinical areas.

## PPE & tools

- Arc-flash label PPE plus the Kestrel minimum (R-PPE-01). Missing or expired label: stop (R-ESC-01).
- CAT III/IV meter and proving unit, voltage-rated gloves, personal locks and tags.
- The site's infection-control gear and permits for clinical areas.
- A list of every room and device fed by the circuits involved.

## Procedure

1. Identify the branch, its transfer switch, and every room and device downstream from the site file and panel labels.
2. Before touching any EES branch, agree the time with the facilities manager and the clinical lead of every affected area, and confirm no procedures are in progress or scheduled (R-HEALTH-01). Give 72-hour written notice, or the site's longer period, with written acknowledgement (R-NOTIFY-01). Reconfirm in person before starting.
3. If the life safety branch or a transfer switch will be out of service, notify the facility manager, confirm whether a fire watch is required, and have alarm monitoring placed on test as the site requires (R-EMERG-01).
4. Call your supervisor before any EES work; it is life-safety work (R-ESC-01).
5. Never de-energize a critical-branch circuit serving a procedure room while the room is occupied, whoever asks (R-HEALTH-01). Reschedule. If a failing part cannot wait, limit yourself to diagnostic testing in full PPE, or do energized work only under an EEWP signed by the Kestrel operations manager and the facility representative (R-EWP-01), with the clinical lead informed.
6. At the agreed time, confirm the room is empty, lock out every source including both normal and EES feeds (R-LOTO-01), and verify absence of voltage live-dead-live (R-VERIFY-01).
7. Do not move loads between branches or add load to an EES branch without engineering review; NEC Art. 517 limits what each branch may serve.
8. LIM alarm: treat it as a warning, not a power loss. Enter only with the charge nurse's permission and follow infection-control rules. Never switch off the isolated power panel or the critical branch serving the room. Record the hazard-current reading.
9. With clinical approval, have clinical staff unplug non-life-support devices one at a time, last connected first, while you watch the reading. Only clinicians touch life-support equipment. Tag the device that clears the alarm for biomedical engineering.
10. Silencing the audible LIM alarm per site procedure is allowed. Never bypass or disconnect the LIM or ground an isolated conductor.
11. If the alarm persists with everything removable unplugged, suspect the fixed wiring or the LIM. Investigate only when the room is empty (R-HEALTH-01) and call your supervisor (R-ESC-01). Press the LIM test button only with the clinical lead's agreement; it must alarm, then return to safe.
12. Restore: remove your locks, re-energize in the agreed order, and with the clinical lead confirm equipment, nurse call, refrigeration alarms and LIM status. Explain only verified findings (R-CUST-01).

## Verification

- Written clinical and facilities agreement on file before work started.
- Absence of voltage proven on every feed to the work.
- LIM shows safe, below the site's threshold (NFPA 99 permits up to 5 mA; many sites set lower).
- Clinical lead confirms the area is ready for patients.

## Stop-work triggers

- A procedure starts, or a patient arrives, in a room whose circuit is about to be opened.
- You cannot confirm which branch or rooms a circuit serves.
- A LIM alarm persists with all devices removed, or isolated wiring is faulted.
- Any request to bypass a LIM, transfer switch or alarm.
- Missing or expired arc-flash label, or a lockout you cannot complete.

## Records

- Notices, acknowledgements and the clinical leads who agreed (R-DOC-01).
- Circuits, rooms and branches affected, with start and end times.
- LIM readings, devices tagged, who was told, and the fire watch decision.
- Before and after photos and readings.
