## When this applies

- Measuring voltage, current or frequency on energized equipment; troubleshooting control circuits; infrared thermography; reading trip-unit or drive fault data with covers removed.
- These tasks are permitted without an EEWP only when performed by a qualified person in full label PPE (R-EWP-01, R-PPE-01).
- Diagnostics end where repair begins. Tightening, replacing parts, landing or lifting conductors, or jumpering terminals is not diagnostics: de-energize (sp-loto) or, rarely, obtain an EEWP (sp-energized-work-permit).

## Hazards

- Shock from contact with exposed parts, or through a damaged lead or glove.
- Arc flash from a slipped probe bridging phases, a meter on the wrong function (amps or ohms across voltage), a dropped tool, loose hardware falling into a bus, or a cover snagging live parts as it comes off.
- Meter failure from transients when the CAT rating is too low for the location.
- Stored energy in drives and capacitors after the input is off.
- The temptation to "just fix it" while energized.

## PPE & tools

- Kestrel minimum (R-PPE-01) plus the PPE and arc rating from the equipment label for every task inside the arc-flash boundary, including cover removal.
- Voltage-rated rubber gloves of the correct class with leather protectors: air test before use and confirm the electrical test date is within six months.
- CAT III/IV meter and clamp meter rated for the voltage; leads with shrouded connectors and tip guards; fused voltage leads for recorders.
- 1000 V insulated hand tools, insulating blanket or barrier for nearby live parts, nonconductive flashlight, barricade tape.
- Infrared camera; use the site's IR windows where installed.
- No conductive jewelry, watches, metal pens or keys.

## Procedure

1. Define what you need to know and why. Check the site file and service history for this equipment and plan the fewest test points that answer the question.
2. Read the arc-flash label. If it is missing, illegible or dated more than 5 years ago, stop: do not open or test the equipment energized; escalate (R-PPE-01, R-ESC-01).
3. Set up: clear the working space, confirm good lighting and dry footing, and barricade at the larger of the limited approach boundary and the arc-flash boundary. Tell the site contact what you are doing (R-CUST-01).
4. Inspect meter, leads, gloves and protectors. Prove the CAT III/IV meter on a known live source, and treat every conductor as energized (R-VERIFY-01).
5. Put on full label PPE before removing any cover. Stand to the side, keep screws captive, and check for wiring attached to the cover before lifting it away.
6. Select the meter function and range before connecting; never change function while connected. Use a clamp for current. Keep your free hand away from the enclosure.
7. Only your gloved hands and the test probes enter the restricted approach boundary. Cover nearby exposed parts you could contact.
8. For thermography, compare similar components under similar load. A rise of more than 15 °C over similar components is an escalation trigger (R-ESC-01).
9. Install recorder voltage leads and current probes de-energized when a shutdown is available. If installed energized, use fused leads on accessible points without loosening any hardware.
10. When you find the fault, stop testing. Plan the repair de-energized (sp-loto). An energized repair needs an EEWP (R-EWP-01).
11. Refit all covers and fasteners before leaving the equipment, even for a short break.
12. If a zero reading led to a decision, re-prove the meter on a known live source.
13. Tell the customer only what you verified and what you will check next; never guess at a cause (R-CUST-01).

## Verification

- Every reading is logged with its location, meter used and load condition at the time.
- Covers, dead fronts and doors are back with all fasteners; nothing left inside the enclosure.
- Barricades come down only once the equipment is closed.

## Stop-work triggers

- Missing, illegible or expired label, or a task needing more PPE than you have (R-PPE-01, R-ESC-01).
- Overheating, burning smell, arcing sounds, damage or water in or on the equipment (R-ESC-01).
- The task drifting from measuring into repair or adjustment.
- Damaged meter, leads or gloves, or a meter not rated for the circuit.
- Poor lighting, a wet floor, or having to reach where you cannot see.
- Customer or schedule pressure to "just fix it live".
- Any task beyond your qualification (R-ESC-01).

## Records

- Readings, infrared temperatures with ambient, breaker sizes and findings in the work order before leaving site (R-DOC-01).
- Photos of the label and the gear before and after (R-DOC-01).
- Any label, overheating or damage concern, with the time the supervisor was called.
