## When this applies

- Any work on exposed energized conductors or circuit parts beyond testing and diagnostics: tightening or retorquing, replacing a breaker or fuse holder, landing or lifting conductors, installing a breaker in a live panel, cleaning or repairing inside live gear (R-EWP-01).
- Testing, measuring and thermography by a qualified person in full label PPE do not need an EEWP (sp-energized-diagnostics).
- The default answer is no. Kestrel plans the job de-energized under sp-loto unless the criteria below are met and the permit is signed.

## Hazards

- Energized repair exposes the worker to shock and arc flash while using exactly the tools, parts and hand movements most likely to start an arc.
- Pressure from customers, tenants and schedules to stay energized for convenience, which is never a justification (R-EWP-01).
- Scope creep: a permitted task growing into unpermitted work.

## PPE & tools

- Kestrel minimum plus full PPE from a valid arc-flash label (R-PPE-01); voltage-rated gloves of the correct class with leather protectors.
- 1000 V insulated tools; insulating blankets, covers or barriers for adjacent live parts; insulated fuse pullers where fuses are handled live (1910.335).
- Barricades and signs, and an attendant where barricades alone are not enough.
- A standby person trained in emergency response, CPR and safe release of a shock victim, who knows how to de-energize the source.

## Procedure

1. Stop and classify the task. Beyond diagnostics means a shutdown or a permit.
2. Look for the shutdown first: schedule an outage with 72-hour written notice (R-NOTIFY-01), bring in temporary power, shift loads, or isolate only the affected section.
3. Test the justification. Only two are acceptable (R-EWP-01):
   - Greater hazard from shutting down, for example interrupting life-support equipment, emergency alarm systems, hazardous-location ventilation, or lighting needed for safe egress.
   - Infeasibility because of equipment design or operational limits, for example a continuous process that would need a full shutdown for one circuit.
   - Customer convenience, lost revenue, overtime cost or tenant complaints are never acceptable.
4. Call your supervisor (R-ESC-01). Life-safety, standby and healthcare loads also follow R-EMERG-01 and R-HEALTH-01.
5. Confirm the equipment has a valid arc-flash label. With a missing, illegible or expired label no permit can proceed (R-PPE-01).
6. Draft the EEWP. It must record:
   - The equipment, its location and the exact task.
   - The justification and the evidence for it.
   - The safe work practices to be used.
   - Shock risk assessment: voltage, limited and restricted approach boundaries, shock PPE.
   - Arc-flash risk assessment: incident energy or PPE category, arc-flash boundary, arc-flash PPE.
   - How unqualified people will be kept out.
   - Confirmation that a job briefing on the specific hazards was held.
7. Obtain both signatures before any work: the Kestrel operations manager and the customer's facility representative (R-EWP-01). A verbal OK, a text message or a single signature is not a permit.
8. Brief everyone involved: task, boundaries, PPE, roles, emergency plan, and that anyone can stop the work.
9. Barricade at the larger of the arc-flash and limited approach boundaries; post the attendant; position the standby person.
10. Cover adjacent energized parts, then perform only the task written on the permit.
11. If the scope, crew or conditions change, stop and obtain a revised permit.
12. Close out: refit covers, remove barriers, record completion on the permit and return it to the operations manager.

## Verification

- Both signatures are on the permit before any cover comes off.
- Each worker's PPE matches the permit and the label.
- Boundaries, barriers, attendant and standby person are in place; briefing recorded.
- The work performed matches the permit scope exactly.

## Stop-work triggers

- A missing signature, or a justification based on cost, convenience or schedule (R-EWP-01).
- Missing, illegible or expired label (R-PPE-01, R-ESC-01).
- Site conditions different from the permit: other equipment, damage, water, overheating (R-ESC-01).
- Scope creep, a crew change without a briefing, or loss of the standby person.
- A task beyond your qualification or comfort; escalating is never a failure (R-ESC-01).

## Records

- Signed EEWP attached to the work order and kept on file (R-DOC-01).
- Photos before and after, readings and torque values (R-DOC-01).
- Customer approvals and any scope changes recorded in the work order (R-CUST-01).
