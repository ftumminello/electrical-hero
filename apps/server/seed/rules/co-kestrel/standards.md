# Kestrel Electric Co. — Field Work Standards (KES-100, Rev. 7)

These standards apply to every Kestrel electrician, apprentice and subcontractor on a customer site. Where a customer's own site rules are stricter, follow the stricter rule. Cite rule IDs in work orders, reports and debriefs.

## R-LOTO-01 — Lockout/tagout before work

- De-energize and lock out every source that can feed the equipment before working on or near exposed conductors. Include backfeeds: generators, the emergency side of transfer switches, UPS outputs, solar, and separately derived control power.
- Each worker applies their own personal lock and danger tag. Never work under someone else's lock.
- Try-out: after locking, attempt to operate the device or start the load to confirm it is isolated.
- Remove only your own lock. Removing a lock whose owner has left site requires the supervisor (R-ESC-01).

## R-VERIFY-01 — Verify absence of voltage

- Treat every conductor as energized until you have proven otherwise.
- Use a CAT III/IV rated meter: test it on a known live source, test every phase-to-phase and phase-to-ground combination at the point of work, then re-test on the known live source (live-dead-live).
- Non-contact voltage testers are for screening only. They are never used to declare a circuit dead.

## R-PPE-01 — PPE and arc-flash labels

- Select PPE from the equipment's arc-flash label (incident energy or PPE category, and arc-flash boundary) for any task inside the boundary, including voltage testing and removing covers on energized gear.
- If the label is missing, illegible, or dated more than 5 years ago, stop: do not open or test that equipment energized. Escalate (R-ESC-01).
- Minimum on every site: safety glasses, hard hat, arc-rated long-sleeve shirt (8 cal/cm² minimum), and voltage-rated gloves with leather protectors for any testing.

## R-EWP-01 — Energized work permit

- Work on exposed energized conductors (anything beyond testing and diagnostics) is prohibited unless an Energized Electrical Work Permit (EEWP) is signed by the Kestrel operations manager and the customer's facility representative.
- The only acceptable justifications are infeasibility or a greater hazard from shutting down (for example, life-support equipment). Customer convenience or lost revenue is never a justification.
- Diagnostic testing, measuring and thermography are permitted without an EEWP when performed by a qualified person in full PPE per R-PPE-01.

## R-NOTIFY-01 — Customer notification before shutdowns

- Planned shutdowns: notify the site contact in writing at least 72 hours ahead with scope, affected loads, start time and expected duration, and get written acknowledgement.
- Emergency de-energization: notify the site contact immediately, before opening the device if it is safe to do so, otherwise within 15 minutes.
- Healthcare and life-safety loads also follow R-HEALTH-01 and R-EMERG-01.

## R-DOC-01 — Documentation

- Photograph every panel or piece of gear you open, before and after the work, with labels visible.
- Record readings (voltages, currents, infrared temperatures), breaker sizes, torque values and findings in the work order before leaving site.

## R-LABEL-01 — Labels and panel schedules

- Any circuit added, removed or changed must be reflected in a typed panel schedule (never handwritten) and labeled at the panel and at the load.
- Leave the updated schedule in the panel door and attach a photo of it to the work order.

## R-LOAD-01 — Adding load

- Before adding any circuit or load, confirm panel and feeder capacity using recent load data or a 30-day recording plus the calculated load of the new equipment. Confirm conductor ampacity and available spaces.
- No double-lugging: never put two conductors under one terminal unless the terminal is listed for it.
- Tandem (duplex) breakers only in positions where the panel label lists them.
- If capacity or space is insufficient, stop and send the customer a written proposal (R-CUST-01). Never "make it fit".

## R-ESC-01 — Escalation

Call your supervisor before proceeding when you find: signs of overheating (discoloration, melted insulation, or an infrared reading more than 15 °C above similar components); damaged or water-intruded equipment; any work on life-safety systems; a missing, illegible or expired arc-flash label; a lockout you cannot complete; or any task beyond your qualification. Escalating is never a failure.

## R-EMERG-01 — Emergency and standby systems

- Never leave a life-safety or legally required standby system out of service without notifying the facility manager and confirming whether a fire watch is required.
- Follow the site's generator test procedure and record transfer times, voltage, frequency and alarms. Report a failed transfer test to the facility within one hour.
- Never manually bypass a transfer switch, or disable generator shutdowns or alarms, without supervisor approval.

## R-HEALTH-01 — Healthcare sites

- Before touching any essential electrical system branch (life safety, critical or equipment), agree the time with the facilities manager and with the clinical lead of every affected area, and confirm no procedures are in progress.
- Never de-energize a critical-branch circuit serving a procedure room while the room is occupied.

## R-CUST-01 — Customer communication

- Be professional and factual. Never guess at a cause in front of the customer: say what you have verified and what you will check next.
- Any change of scope or extra work needs the customer's approval before you do it, recorded in the work order.
