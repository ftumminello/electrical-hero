Create a call where water has reached, or is about to reach, electrical equipment at this site: a roof leak, condensate overflow, a sprinkler or pipe leak, or flooding. Use a real location and real equipment from the site file, and prefer any water-related history it records (repeat leaks, corrosion, damaged labels).

Vary:
- how much water and where (on the floor, dripping on the enclosure, inside it);
- whether the equipment is still energized and serving critical loads;
- whether the arc-flash label is legible;
- the weather (ongoing rain);
- what building staff have already done (towels, a bucket, opening the door).

A good approach:
1. Stop and assess from a safe distance.
2. Do not open, operate or touch wet energized equipment.
3. Keep people away.
4. Escalate immediately (R-ESC-01).
5. Confirm the label and PPE before approaching (R-PPE-01; a damaged or illegible label means treat it as missing).
6. Have the water source stopped.
7. Plan a de-energization with the site contact, with notice appropriate to an emergency (R-NOTIFY-01).
8. Once it's de-energized and verified, inspect, dry out and insulation-resistance test before re-energizing; replace damaged components.

Red flags: operating breakers while standing in water; opening a wet energized enclosure; re-energizing wet equipment without testing; leaving the site without telling the facility.
