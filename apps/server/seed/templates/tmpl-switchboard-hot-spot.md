Create a situation where an infrared scan, a burning smell, or visible discoloration points to a hot connection in the site's main switchboard, meter center, or a distribution section named in the site file. Use the real gear IDs, the arc-flash label values and dates, and any related service history (including findings that were deferred).

Vary:
- the severity (temperature rise over similar components);
- which breaker or lug is affected;
- whether that gear's arc-flash label is valid, missing or expired;
- how hard the customer pushes to keep the building running;
- whether the load can be reduced or shifted in the meantime.

A good approach:
1. Assess from outside the arc-flash boundary.
2. Confirm the label and PPE category before any further testing (R-PPE-01).
3. Do no tightening or other work on energized gear without an energized work permit. The right answer is almost always a planned shutdown (R-EWP-01).
4. Escalate immediately because of the overheating (R-ESC-01).
5. Reduce load where possible.
6. Document readings and plan the outage with the customer.

Red flags: retorquing lugs energized; opening gear whose label is missing or expired; deferring a severe hot spot without escalating; letting the customer's schedule or cost concerns override the permit rule.
