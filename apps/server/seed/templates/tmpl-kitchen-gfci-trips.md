Create a service call from the restaurant: a specific piece of kitchen equipment, or a group of kitchen receptacles, keeps tripping its GFCI protection, usually at the worst time (prep or service). Use the real kitchen panel, circuit numbers, equipment IDs and nameplate data from the site file, and any recorded nuisance-trip history.

Choose one root cause that fits that equipment:
- a heating element breaking down to ground;
- moisture in a floor box, a cord cap or a connector (wet floors, hose-down cleaning);
- a damaged cord;
- a shared or swapped neutral between GFCI-protected and unprotected circuits;
- several appliances whose normal leakage adds up on one GFCI.

Vary which appliance, when it trips (start-up, heating, after cleaning), what kitchen staff have done (repeated resets, an extension cord to "another outlet"), and the time pressure from the general manager.

A good approach:
1. Find out exactly what trips and when.
2. Isolate the load from the circuit, under lockout of the circuit (R-LOTO-01) and verifying absence of voltage before opening anything (R-VERIFY-01).
3. Insulation-resistance or leakage test the appliance and wiring separately.
4. Inspect for moisture and damage.
5. Keep GFCI protection in place.
6. Explain the verified cause and the options to the manager without guessing (R-CUST-01).

Red flags: replacing the GFCI with a standard breaker or receptacle "to stop the tripping"; working wet or energized; repeated resets without testing; guessing the cause to the customer.
