## Adopted codes & effective dates

- **In force (checked 2026-10-07):** NFPA 70-2023 with Annexes A–C, errata and TIAs (WAC 296-46B-010(1)). Chapter 296-46B WAC controls over the NEC when they conflict.
- **Next:** WSR 25-23-069 adopts NFPA 70-2026. It replaces the 2023 edition on **2026-12-31**.
- **Which edition applies:** the edition in effect on the **electrical permit issue date**. New dwellings go by the building-permit date instead. Plan-review projects go by the date L&I received and accepted the plans (010(1)). A commercial permit issued 2026-12-30 is a 2023 NEC job. One issued on or after 2026-12-31 is a 2026 NEC job.
- **Pending, not final:** L&I's 2026-cycle amendments to chapter 296-46B (AO 25-31).
  - CR-101 was WSR 26-01-100. The CR-102 was filed 2026-08-04 (its WSR number is unconfirmed), with a hearing scheduled for 2026-09-14. L&I plans to adopt on 2026-11-17, effective 2026-12-31.
  - Proposals: load-calculation rules move to a new -120, energy storage systems need a design review (new -706), and listed HVAC equipment is exempt from the new GFCI rule until 2028-06-30. None of this is law until a CR-103 is filed.
- Also pending: EVSE and exam rules (WSR 26-15-061).

## Authority, permits & inspections

- **Who inspects:** L&I statewide, except in cities that enforce an equal or stricter ordinance of their own (RCW 19.28.141).
  - L&I's list of those cities: Aberdeen, Bellevue, Bellingham, Burien, Des Moines, Everett, Federal Way, Kirkland, Lacey, Lynnwood, Marysville, Mercer Island, Milton, Mountlake Terrace, Normandy Park, Olympia, Port Angeles, Redmond, Renton, Sammamish, SeaTac, Seattle, Spokane, Tukwila, Vancouver.
  - Tacoma Power's service area is also excluded.
  - **Port Calder, Brookhaven Falls and Lindale Heights are not on the list.** Buy permits from L&I (My L&I/EPIS) and request L&I inspections.
- **Permit rules (WAC 296-46B-901):**
  - Post the permit, or its number at the panel feeding the work, before starting.
  - Each firm needs its own permit. Two firms may never share one.
  - No inspection, energizing or utility connection without a valid permit.
  - A permit expires one year after purchase.
- **No permit needed (Class A):** like-in-kind replacement of lamps, one set of fuses, contactors, relays, starters, branch-circuit breakers, up to five switches or receptacles, or one motor of 10 hp or less. Replacing a whole equipment unit is not exempt.
- **Class B label (WAC 296-46B-908):** random inspection for minor work. Validate the label online before starting.
- **Inspection timing:**
  - Request inspection within 3 working days of finishing, or within 1 working day of energizing any part, whichever comes first (901(9)).
  - Never conceal work before approval. Correct violations within 15 days.
  - Inspectors may disconnect dangerous installations. Reconnecting without approval is unlawful (RCW 19.28.101).
- **Plan review (WAC 296-46B-900):** required before work starts in educational, institutional and health-care occupancies, with exemptions for small work.

## Licensing & supervision

- **Firm:** an electrical contractor license is required to advertise, bid or do electrical work. It needs a $4,000 bond and lasts 24 months (RCW 19.28.041).
  - The firm names a full-time master electrician or administrator, responsible for code compliance, safety procedures, permits and correction notices (RCW 19.28.061(5)).
- **Workers:** must hold a master, journey level, specialty or training certificate (RCW 19.28.161(1)).
  - **General journey level (01)** covers all electrical work (WAC 296-46B-920(1)).
  - The 01 exam requires 8,000 hours, 4,000 of them on new commercial or industrial work (RCW 19.28.191).
  - Certificates renew every 3 years, with 8 hours of continuing education per year (RCW 19.28.211; WAC 296-46B-940).
  - Wear the certificate visibly on the front of the upper body. It may go under arc-flash gear. Carry photo ID (WAC 296-46B-940(3)).
- **Trainees (RCW 19.28.161; WAC 296-46B-942):**
  - A trainee carries a training certificate, renewed every 2 years with 48 classroom hours.
  - **Supervision means being on the same job site.** Separate buildings at one address do not count, except a single-family home (WAC 296-46B-100).
  - **Ratios:** journey-level work allows 1 trainee per certified 01 electrician. Specialty work allows 2 per electrician. Enrolled college or trade-school students allow up to 4. At 1:3 or 1:4 the electrician may only supervise and instruct, not install, and must be on site all day.
  - Otherwise the supervisor must be on site at least 75% of each working day.
  - An 01 candidate with more than 7,000 supervised hours who is in or has finished an approved program may get a six-month unsupervised certificate (942(16)).
- **EVSE on public works:** since 2026-01-01, people doing electrical work on EVSE for public works must hold EVITP or equivalent certification (RCW 19.28.285).

## State amendments that matter on commercial jobs

Paraphrased; check the WAC text for exact wording.

- **Existing systems:** may stay as they are. Any addition, alteration or repair must meet current rules (WAC 296-46B-010(14)).
- **Schools and institutions:** wiring is limited to raceway, MI, MC or AC cable (010(12)).
- **Disconnect ID:** NEC 110.22 marking requires an identification plate. Breakers listed on a panel schedule are exempt. In non-dwellings the plate also names the source panelboard (WAC 296-46B-110(3)).
- **Non-dwelling GFCI:** not required for three-phase receptacles unless another NEC rule requires it. For NEC 210.8(B), "kitchen" means any area where dishes are washed or food is prepared (WAC 296-46B-210(3)).
- **Ground-fault protection of equipment:** must be performance-tested before going into service. The signed test record, with trip settings, must be on site for inspection (210(5), 215(3), 230(12)).
- **One-line diagram:** required for non-dwelling services or feeders over 400 A or over 600 V on jobs without plan review (WAC 296-46B-215(2)).
  - Signed by the master/administrator or stamped by an engineer.
  - Shows available fault current, short-circuit ratings, demand loads and panel schedules. Keep it on site.
- **Ampacity plate:** required where conductors are rated below their overcurrent protection or equipment rating (215(1), 230(6)).
- **Services (WAC 296-46B-230):**
  - Check the utility's requirements first.
  - Inside a building, service conductors may run only in RMC, IMC, wireway, busway, gutter, Schedule 40 PVC or heavier, cablebus or MI. Other methods are allowed only with supply-side overcurrent protection.
  - An outside disconnect goes on the building, or within sight of it and within 15 ft, with an identification plate. Inside, the service raceway may extend no more than 15 ft into the building.
- **Grounding (WAC 296-46B-250):** new buildings on permanent concrete foundations need a concrete-encased electrode. Rod, pipe or plate electrodes need two, at least 6 ft apart, unless a test shows 25 ohms or less.
- **Panelboards:** service equipment and subpanels must be readily accessible. They may never be in clothes closets, toilet rooms or shower rooms (WAC 296-46B-408).

## Workplace safety rules

- Washington runs an OSHA-approved State Plan: L&I's Division of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH) enforces WISHA (RCW 49.17) for private and state/local-government employers.
  - Federal baseline: 29 CFR 1910 Subpart S (1910.333) and 1926 Subpart K (1926.417). Cite the WAC equivalents.
- **General industry** (service work in operating facilities), WAC 296-24 Part L (296-24-957 to -990):
  - De-energize before work unless that is infeasible or adds hazards (296-24-975(1)).
  - Lockout/tagout steps (296-24-975(2)):
    - keep written procedures;
    - put a lock and tag on each disconnect used (tag only under strict conditions);
    - never rely on interlocks or push buttons as the only means;
    - release stored energy;
    - a qualified person tests for absence of voltage, including backfeed, and checks the tester before and after any test over 600 V.
  - Only qualified persons may work on energized parts (296-24-960); training per 296-24-970.
  - Provide insulated tools, nonconductive hard hats and eye/face protection (296-24-980).
- **Construction** (new work and remodels), WAC 296-155 Part I (296-155-426 to -462):
  - De-energize and ground, or guard, any circuit a worker could contact. Locate underground lines first. Keep 10 ft from overhead lines of 50 kV or less (296-155-428).
  - Lock and tag every point where a circuit can be energized, under written procedures (296-155-429).
- **WAC 296-803, Lockout/tagout (control of hazardous energy):** covers servicing machines and equipment. It excludes construction and electrical-work hazards covered by Part L. A 296-803 program satisfies Part L only if it addresses electrical hazards and adds Part L's tag and verification steps.
- **Accident prevention program:** every employer needs a written one (WAC 296-800-140; construction: 296-155-110).
- NFPA 70E: adoption by Washington rule is (unconfirmed). Grade on the WAC duties above.
